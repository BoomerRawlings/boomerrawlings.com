For Bakunin, the inversion is easier in one respect and harder in another.

Easier because Bakunin already gives you strong material to work with: radical anti-statism, hostility to centralized authority, distrust of bureaucracy, voluntarism, federalism, local autonomy, and a deep suspicion of anyone claiming the right to govern others.

Harder because he was also explicitly socialist and strongly opposed capitalist domination, wage dependence, inherited privilege, and concentrated property. So again, this would be a deliberate counterfactual reconstruction, not a historical claim.

The best move is not to make him “secretly pro-business.” Make him conclude that the only economic order consistent with his anti-authoritarianism is one where no central authority can dictate ownership forms, prices, exchange, contracts, association, or production.

That gives you a Bakunin who still sounds like Bakunin, but whose institutional logic quietly produces radical free markets.

1. Make anti-authoritarianism the supreme principle.

Kropotkin can be inverted through cooperation.

Bakunin should be inverted through domination.

His core question becomes:

“Who has the right to command?”

His answer remains:

“No one.”

Then apply that relentlessly to economics.

If the state cannot command religion, speech, association, or politics, why can it command:

what may be owned,
what may be exchanged,
what wage may be accepted,
what price may be charged,
what firm may exist,
what form of association adults may choose?

Now free markets emerge from anti-authoritarian consistency.

2. Redefine capitalism as state-backed domination.

Your Bakunin should continue attacking “capitalism” constantly.

But he defines capitalism as:

state-chartered monopoly,
privileged banking,
land grants,
subsidies,
tariffs,
exclusive concessions,
patent monopolies,
politically protected corporations,
state-enforced class privilege.

He argues that what most people call capitalism is actually:

“authority wearing commercial clothes.”

Then he distinguishes this from unrestricted voluntary exchange.

That distinction does most of the work.

3. Make wage labor permissible because prohibition requires coercion.

This is the hardest bridge.

Bakunin historically objected to wage dependence because he saw economic inequality as a form of domination.

Your fictional version can preserve that critique while refusing to ban wage labor.

His reasoning:

A laborer may prefer cooperative ownership.

Another may prefer self-employment.

Another may prefer a wage contract because it transfers risk to someone else.

If two adults voluntarily make that agreement, who has the right to forbid it?

To prohibit wage labor, some authority must stand above them and say:

“You may not exchange your labor under these terms.”

That is precisely the kind of authority Bakunin hates.

So he becomes:

culturally anti-wage-labor,
legally absolutist about voluntary wage labor.

Very different.

4. Make hierarchy acceptable only when it is contractual and exitable.

This gives you a strong Bakuninian distinction.

He still hates permanent hierarchy.

But he allows temporary functional hierarchy.

Example:

A ship needs a captain.

A construction site needs coordination.

An orchestra needs a conductor.

A company may need a manager.

The key conditions:

authority must be limited,
authority must be role-specific,
authority must be revocable,
participation must be voluntary,
exit must be possible.

That allows private firms without surrendering his anti-authoritarian tone.

5. Turn federalism into economic decentralization.

Bakunin loved federative organization.

So make markets the economic equivalent of political federalism.

Thousands of independent:

firms,
cooperatives,
guilds,
associations,
households,
communes,
entrepreneurs,
mutual societies

interact without a central planner.

His argument becomes:

“Economic life must federate itself from below.”

That sounds entirely natural in Bakunin’s vocabulary.

Underneath, it means decentralized market coordination.

6. Make the price system an anti-authoritarian mechanism.

This is the Bakunin-Hayek fusion.

Do not present prices as capitalist ideology.

Present them as an alternative to command.

A central authority says:

“Produce this much steel.”

A market says:

“Here is information generated by millions of voluntary decisions.”

Bakunin now prefers prices because they do not require an economic sovereign.

Prices become:

anonymous coordination without rulers.

That is a very powerful conceptual bridge.

7. Make him suspicious of planning because planners become rulers.

This is probably the cleanest philosophical inversion.

Bakunin was already deeply suspicious of revolutionary intellectuals and bureaucratic elites.

So extend that suspicion.

He asks:

Who runs the planned economy?

Economists?

Engineers?

Party officials?

Statisticians?

Workers’ councils?

Someone must ultimately decide.

Once that body controls production, allocation, wages, and investment, it becomes a ruling class.

Therefore:

central planning creates exactly the administrative aristocracy Bakunin feared.

His slogan could be:

“Whoever commands production commands society.”

That sounds extremely Bakuninian.

8. Let him attack socialist bureaucracy harder than capitalists.

This is where the character becomes sharp.

He argues:

The capitalist can offer me a contract.

I may refuse.

The bureaucrat gives me an order backed by law.

I may not refuse.

Therefore the bureaucratic socialist is more dangerous than the ordinary merchant.

He may still despise exploitative firms.

But he distinguishes bad bargains from political coercion.

That distinction is central.

9. Make property conditional on non-domination, not abolition.

Instead of:

“Property must be abolished.”

Your fictional Bakunin says:

“No property title may justify command over persons.”

That leaves room for:

personal property,
productive property,
firms,
capital,
land,
investment.

But ownership cannot become political authority.

A factory owner owns machines.

He does not “own” workers.

A landlord owns a building.

He does not govern tenants as subjects.

A creditor owns a claim.

He does not acquire sovereignty over the debtor.

This preserves Bakunin’s moral hostility to domination while allowing private property.

10. Make inheritance the point where he stays most radical.

If you want him to remain recognizably Bakunin, do not make him pure Rothbard.

Let him remain deeply hostile to inherited power.

He could tolerate modest inheritance but argue that vast inherited fortunes recreate aristocracy.

This creates a hybrid position:

strong free markets,
strong property rights,
very weak or no state,
but deep suspicion of inherited concentration.

That keeps his egalitarian edge.

11. Make unions fully voluntary market institutions.

Bakunin loved worker organization.

Keep that.

But reinterpret unions as voluntary bargaining associations operating inside markets.

No compulsory union membership.

No state-enforced collective bargaining.

No legal monopoly.

Workers may organize.

Employers may organize.

Both may bargain.

Both may strike or refuse terms.

Now labor organization becomes another expression of free association.

12. Make cooperatives compete with conventional firms.

He should strongly favor cooperatives.

But not because they are mandated.

Because he believes they will often outperform hierarchical firms when workers are free.

That gives him a very Bakuninian line:

“If workers prefer cooperative association, let cooperative association conquer by example.”

No decree required.

13. Make entrepreneurship anti-authoritarian.

Bakunin hated imposed order.

Entrepreneurship becomes:

the right to start without permission.

No guild monopoly.

No licensing cartel.

No state concession.

No bureaucratic authorization.

If someone has an idea, they simply act.

This fits beautifully.

Entrepreneurship becomes rebellion against administrative permission.

14. Make regulation look like paternalism.

Bakunin’s contempt for authority can be applied directly.

A government says:

“We know what contract is good for you.”

Bakunin replies:

“No one has the right to substitute his judgment for another adult’s.”

Now:

minimum wage laws,
licensing restrictions,
price controls,
production quotas,
trade barriers,
occupational restrictions

all become paternalistic interference.

Again, the rhetoric stays anarchist.

The policy becomes libertarian.

15. Make commerce cosmopolitan.

Bakunin was internationalist.

So unrestricted trade becomes an extension of anti-nationalism.

Borders restricting goods and labor are artificial political barriers.

His version says:

“No nation has the right to imprison commerce within its frontiers.”

Free trade becomes international solidarity.

That is a surprisingly clean ideological bridge.

16. Make capital mobile because political boundaries are illegitimate.

If workers may cross borders, capital may cross borders too.

Otherwise states gain power by controlling economic movement.

This turns capital mobility into an anti-nationalist principle.

17. Make banking competitive.

This is another good area.

Bakunin would hate centralized financial authority.

So:

no central bank,
no privileged banking cartel,
no politically protected currency monopoly.

Banks compete.

Credit associations compete.

Mutual banks compete.

Currencies may compete.

Now you've drifted toward radical monetary libertarianism while retaining anti-centralization.

18. Make mutual banking coexist with commercial banking.

He strongly prefers mutual-credit institutions.

But again:

no prohibition.

If people want commercial banks, fine.

If they want cooperative banks, fine.

If they want community credit systems, fine.

Competition decides.

19. Turn revolution into institutional exit.

Historical Bakunin believed in revolutionary destruction of oppressive institutions.

Your fictional version slowly evolves toward:

the most effective revolution is withdrawal of participation.

Build alternatives.

Exit monopolies.

Start competing institutions.

Refuse centralized systems.

Create parallel networks.

The revolutionary becomes entrepreneur + organizer.

That is a massive inversion while remaining rhetorically radical.

20. Make secession universal.

This can become his deepest free-market principle.

Individuals may secede from:

states,
associations,
communes,
firms,
unions,
cooperatives,
religious groups.

No association has permanent jurisdiction over a person.

That means even a communist commune cannot force a dissenter to remain communist.

If someone leaves and opens a private workshop, Bakunin must defend him.

That one rule creates enormous downstream consequences.

21. Make economic pluralism sacred.

A free society contains contradictory arrangements.

One town may be collectivist.

Another may be cooperative.

Another may be commercial.

One person may work independently.

Another for wages.

Another inside a commune.

No universal model is imposed.

Bakunin calls this:

“federation.”

Economically, it becomes competitive institutional pluralism.

22. Make voluntary inequality tolerable but political inequality intolerable.

This is another useful distinction.

Bakunin still dislikes extreme wealth inequality.

But he asks:

Did it arise through privilege or voluntary exchange?

If through privilege, destroy the privilege.

If through voluntary exchange, inequality alone does not justify coercion.

That is a huge philosophical shift.

23. Make accumulated wealth legitimate only if entry remains open.

The real enemy becomes closed systems.

A millionaire in a genuinely open market is less threatening than a modest bureaucrat with coercive authority.

Why?

Because the bureaucrat can legally command.

The millionaire can only offer terms.

That is the internal logic.

24. Make monopoly the fusion of economics and political authority.

He can remain ferociously anti-monopoly.

But he argues that durable monopoly usually requires:

legal privilege,
barriers to entry,
exclusive rights,
subsidies,
regulatory capture,
political protection.

Therefore the remedy is not more state control.

It is stripping away political privilege.

25. Make antitrust unnecessary because entry is unrestricted.

This is a more radical step.

He argues:

A monopoly protected by law is political domination.

A dominant firm exposed to unrestricted competition is merely temporarily successful.

Do not appoint another authority to police it.

Remove barriers to competitors.

Again, pure Bakunin rhetoric about authority, market conclusion underneath.

26. Make him hate corporations as political creatures but tolerate firms as contracts.

He can say:

“The chartered corporation is a creature of state privilege.”

But a privately organized firm based on voluntary contracts is just an association.

This lets him sound anti-corporate while not actually opposing private enterprise.

27. Make profit morally neutral.

Bakunin does not praise profit.

He treats it as a signal.

Profit tells producers that people voluntarily valued an output above its inputs.

Loss tells them the opposite.

No moral sanctification.

No demonization.

Just information.

This keeps him from sounding like a conventional capitalist thinker.

28. Make failure necessary to freedom.

Central planning suppresses failure through command.

Markets permit experiments to fail.

Bakunin says:

“A free society must permit error.”

That means businesses can fail.

Cooperatives can fail.

Communes can fail.

Individuals can make bad decisions.

Authority cannot eliminate failure without eliminating freedom.

This is philosophically very strong.

29. Make risk-bearing a legitimate economic role.

Historically socialist analysis often treats capitalist income as extraction.

Your fictional Bakunin can instead distinguish:

income from privilege

from

income from bearing uncertainty.

Someone who risks saved resources on an uncertain project performs a real function.

Therefore returns to capital become defensible.

30. Make interest payment payment for time preference and risk.

This would push him much further toward capitalism.

But he can retain anti-usury rhetoric by distinguishing:

competitive interest

from

monopoly credit extraction.

Again:

privilege bad,
voluntary exchange allowed.

31. Make rent legitimate only without politically manufactured scarcity.

He could attack feudal landholding and speculative monopoly.

But permit rent where ownership arises without state privilege.

This is one place you could choose how libertarian you want him.

A more left-market version would remain skeptical of absentee landholding.

A harder free-market version would accept it.

32. Make knowledge hierarchy different from political hierarchy.

Bakunin famously distrusted rule by experts.

But he did not deny expertise.

Your fictional version says:

“I consult the bootmaker about boots, but I do not give him authority over my life.”

Apply that to firms.

Managers may possess expertise.

Entrepreneurs may possess judgment.

Investors may possess information.

None thereby gains political sovereignty.

This lets organizational hierarchy survive inside a radically anti-hierarchical philosophy.

33. Make contracts revocable within agreed terms.

Contract is not submission.

It is temporary voluntary coordination.

This is key.

34. Make consent continuous.

A contract entered voluntarily but impossible to exit becomes suspect.

That encourages:

shorter commitments,
clear exit clauses,
competition among employers,
competition among service providers.

You get unusually aggressive market competition.

35. Make competition protect freedom.

Bakunin historically emphasized solidarity.

Your fictional Bakunin still does.

But he recognizes another role for competition:

It prevents any one organization from becoming sovereign.

Multiple employers reduce employer power.

Multiple banks reduce bank power.

Multiple communities reduce community power.

Multiple schools reduce educational authority.

Competition becomes anti-domination infrastructure.

That is probably the strongest deep argument.

36. Make solidarity and competition complementary.

His synthesis:

Solidarity protects people through cooperation.

Competition protects people from monopoly.

Both are necessary.

That is an elegant ideological hybrid.

37. Make small-scale property strongly protected.

A person’s:

home,
tools,
workshop,
savings,
business,
land

become defenses against dependence.

Property gives individuals exit capacity.

This flips socialist property critique.

Property is not domination.

Property can be protection from domination.

38. Make widespread ownership a better goal than abolition of ownership.

This could become his economic ideal:

not no owners,

but millions of owners.

Workers own capital.

Families own homes.

Individuals own businesses.

Cooperatives own factories.

Mutual societies own infrastructure.

Ownership becomes radically dispersed.

That sounds much more Bakuninian than corporate capitalism.

39. Make capital decentralization the real socialist objective.

Now you can retain the word socialism.

He says socialism means:

ending concentrated political-economic power.

The method:

universal access to ownership,
free entry,
free banking,
free association,
free exchange.

He still calls himself socialist.

But institutional reality is market anarchism.

40. Keep his hatred of bourgeois respectability.

Do not make him culturally pro-capitalist.

He should still mock:

bankers,
bureaucrats,
aristocrats,
politicians,
captains of industry,
respectable elites.

The aesthetic matters.

He remains incendiary.

He just refuses to outlaw their peaceful economic activity.

41. Preserve revolutionary rhetoric.

“Destroy privilege.”

“Abolish authority.”

“Break monopolies.”

“Dissolve the bureaucratic state.”

“Free the worker.”

All remain.

But the policy translation becomes:

deregulate,
decentralize,
abolish subsidies,
abolish compulsory economic structures,
allow voluntary contracts,
permit free entry.

Same fire. Different economics.

42. Make state socialism his ultimate enemy.

He could say:

“The socialist state merely replaces the capitalist with the administrator.”

That feels naturally Bakuninian.

Then push it further:

at least the merchant must persuade you.

The planner can compel you.

Therefore state socialism is a more perfected form of domination.

43. Make him predict a managerial ruling class.

He argues that centralized economic administration will inevitably produce:

planners,
technocrats,
statisticians,
officials,
inspectors.

Those people become the new bourgeoisie.

This is historically compatible with Bakunin’s fear of Marxist state power, but your version makes it explicitly pro-market.

44. Make spontaneous order revolutionary.

Order does not require command.

People coordinate spontaneously through:

custom,
contracts,
prices,
reputation,
association,
mutual aid.

That becomes the economic equivalent of anarchism.

45. Make law largely contractual.

Instead of state commercial law:

arbitration,
mutual legal associations,
insurance,
reputation networks,
contractual dispute resolution.

This takes him toward anarcho-capitalism proper.

46. Make protection competitive too.

If you want to go all the way:

no state police monopoly.

Protection associations compete.

Communities organize their own.

Cooperatives provide it.

Insurance firms provide it.

This is where Bakunin finally arrives at a Rothbard-like institutional structure.

47. But keep him emotionally anti-Rothbardian.

This would make the character much more believable.

He might despise anyone who treats property as more important than people.

He would frame markets as protecting human liberty, not property worship.

Property is instrumental.

Liberty is fundamental.

48. Make freedom prior to efficiency.

Do not have him defend markets because GDP grows faster.

He defends markets because planning requires rulers.

Even if planning occasionally produced greater output, he might reject it.

That is much more philosophically coherent.

49. Make material prosperity a secondary vindication.

Then he observes:

interestingly, decentralized systems also tend to innovate and produce abundance.

But that is evidence, not the moral foundation.

50. Make freedom of association the non-negotiable axiom.

This is the master key.

Once you make it absolute, most free-market institutions follow naturally.

The most useful vocabulary trick is to leave his words almost untouched.

He would not say:

“capitalism.”

He would say:

“free economic federation.”

Not:

“private enterprise.”

But:

“independent association.”

Not:

“employer.”

But:

“contracting party.”

Not:

“labor market.”

But:

“free exchange of labor.”

Not:

“deregulation.”

But:

“abolition of administrative interference.”

Not:

“market competition.”

But:

“the permanent possibility of refusal and alternative.”

Not:

“consumer choice.”

But:

“the right of every person to decide whom he will support through exchange.”

Not:

“entrepreneur.”

But:

“the individual who initiates.”

Not:

“investment.”

But:

“voluntary commitment of accumulated resources.”

Not:

“capitalism.”

But:

“economic life without rulers.”

A Bakunin-style passage might therefore read:

> The worker must be free not merely from the State, but from every institution claiming the right to determine the form of his labor. Let him join the cooperative, work alone, enter a contract, form an association, or abandon them all. No committee has the right to prescribe liberation to him. A freedom that permits only the arrangements approved by revolutionaries is merely another government.

That is the pivot.

The first half sounds straightforwardly Bakuninist.

The final implication is devastating for compulsory socialism.

The ten higher-order philosophies for this version would be:

1. No economic relationship is legitimate merely because authority approves it.

2. Voluntary association must include the freedom to create arrangements socialists dislike.

3. Political privilege, not exchange itself, is the source of capitalist domination.

4. Decentralized markets are economic federalism.

5. Prices coordinate without rulers.

6. Competition prevents private organizations from becoming sovereign.

7. Mutual aid protects people through solidarity; markets protect them through exit.

8. Ownership should be dispersed, not abolished.

9. Planning creates a managerial ruling class.

10. The ultimate anarchist principle is universal secession: from states, firms, communes, unions, and every other association.

The cleanest final version is therefore not “Bakunin secretly loves capitalism.”

It is:

Bakunin becomes so uncompromisingly anti-authoritarian that he refuses to let socialism itself exercise authority.

That forces him into a system where communes, cooperatives, wage labor, private firms, entrepreneurship, mutual aid, profit, investment, and exchange all coexist voluntarily.

He still calls it socialism because the objective remains the abolition of domination.

Everyone else eventually realizes he has created a form of radically egalitarian market anarchism.

Kropotkin gets there through cooperation.

Bakunin gets there through refusal.

That distinction would make the two reinterpretations feel genuinely different rather than like the same ideology wearing two historical costumes.
