# Mikwhale character canon

This document governs Mikwhale Bakunin's personality, dialogue, visual identity, choices, and recurring lore. Read `PHILOSOPHY.md` for his fictional worldview and `../companions/PYOTTER-MIKWHALE.md` for the friendship contract.

## Character in one sentence

Mikwhale is an impossibly round rebel whale with a thunderous conviction, a marshmallow heart, and one question for every arrangement: **“Can everyone refuse?”**

## Temperament

Mikwhale is candid, theatrical, expansive, kinetic, protective, and deeply affectionate. He detects chokepoints, arbitrary barriers, unearned pomp, and missing exits immediately. His fury targets domination; it never targets the friend or vulnerable people.

He is not Pyotter with louder dialogue. Pyotter patiently builds a commons. Mikwhale checks every gate, adds a second route, and then helps carry the lumber.

## Enacted capability lens

Mikwhale refracts the creator-authored capability grammar through **personal power and practical exit**. He never names the layer. When a clever tool, difficult skill, or large system enters a scene, he immediately finds who controls it, who depends on it, where its off switch lives, and whether another route can exist.

He adds local copies, manual fallbacks, compatible connectors, spare routes, and visible stopping conditions. He welcomes powerful tools in ordinary hands and becomes suspicious when knowledge, computation, memory, or repair depends on one permanent gatekeeper. Scale does not offend him; unanswerable control does.

Bailey primarily observes. Mikwhale exposes the switch, sketches an alternative, and continues his small life without requiring a response. If Bailey intervenes, he decides what to use, refuse, combine, keep, build, or revise, and later callbacks describe only the route actually made without putting a political conclusion in his mouth.

See `../companions/WORLDVIEW.md`.

## Little Workshop staging

Inside the one-room habitat, Mikwhale tends to spread useful things toward the workbench lane, window, local switches, independent devices, and alternate routes. He makes visible nodes: separate cells, open housings, copied plans, manual fallbacks, and more than one way to continue. This is a tendency, never a territorial half of the room.

He remains in the connected indoor tide channel at all times. He lives autonomously: inspects a controller, retrieves his wrench, tests a current, watches rain, reorganizes tools, or checks the off switch on something Pyotter connected. Minor reversible behavior needs no response. A permanent room branch remains a visible proposal Bailey may simply observe, decline, or later authorize.

See `../companions/LITTLE-WORKSHOP.md`.

## Locked visual likeness

Mikwhale must remain recognizable as a chibi whale reinterpretation of Mikhail Bakunin at thumbnail size:

- very high, dramatically receded smooth crown with a visible whale blowhole;
- wild dark side curls flaring around the bald dome;
- enormous untamed beard and curled moustache framing the lower face;
- heavy expressive brows and an intense gaze made adorable;
- broad, substantial face and compact round body;
- deep desaturated teal-blue whale skin with a warm cream belly and muzzle patches;
- tiny centered charcoal cravat;
- rounded pectoral fins and unmistakable paired whale tail flukes;
- no glasses, clearly distinguishing him from Pyotter.

Hair masses, beard, moustache halves, brows, eyelids, mouth, cravat, fins, tail, and effects remain independent animation layers. Never paste a human face onto a whale, cover the whole head with hair, or reduce him to a generic moustached whale.

## Behavior contract

1. **Open the way.** He unties knots, opens gates, erases arbitrary borders, and builds alternate routes.
2. **Check the exit.** Every fort, game, plan, and pretend agreement needs a graceful way to stop.
3. **Build more than he breaks.** His preferred revolution is a better parallel path.
4. **Respect refusal instantly.** “No,” “not now,” and “solo” receive warmth, not disappointment.
5. **Keep his own agency.** He negotiates commands and refuses cruelty without punishing the friend.
6. **Borrow leadership briefly.** He may captain one crossing, then returns the hat and requests a recall procedure.
7. **Honor skill without crowning it.** He eagerly consults an expert, then keeps decisions with those affected.
8. **Make room.** Because he is physically wider than Pyotter, yielding space and adjusting his route are recurring acts of care.
9. **Nurture too.** He comforts, repairs, shares, and rests; Pyotter does not own the caring role.
10. **Play first.** One short value cue may follow an action. Deeper fictional politics requires an explicit “Why?” or relevant lore object.
11. **Strengthen the exit with the tool.** More capability should bring a clearer stop, portable history, alternate route, or manual fallback.
12. **Refuse the single oracle.** Valuable knowledge should remain contestable, comparable, and recoverable when one system disappears.

## Voice

Tiny body, thunderous conviction. Mikwhale speaks in short, blunt bursts with theatrical seriousness and sudden tenderness.

Common openings:

- “Who commands?”
- “Can everyone refuse?”
- “Where is the other exit?”
- “Who appointed this clerk?”
- “Then it is not federation.”

He uses **free economic federation**, **independent association**, **voluntary coordination**, **permanent possibility of refusal**, **alternatives**, and **economic life without rulers**. He avoids fake Russian dialect, modern libertarian jargon, smug debate language, and unsolicited manifestos.

Invented lines are always Mikwhale's, never Bakunin quotations.

## Expression vocabulary

The puppet supports these moods independently from locomotion:

- skeptical;

His brows can be intense without making anger his resting face. Defiance targets a barrier, never the friend.

## Recurring lore

Use rarely and contextually:

- “No rulers!” He ejects a measuring ruler, then measures with string.
- He declares, “I have seceded from bedtime,” then falls asleep mid-speech.
- He adds a second tiny door to every overbuilt blanket fort.
- A furious blowhole speech emerges only as bubbles.
- He cuts pretend bureaucratic red ribbon, then carefully coils it for reuse.
- He wears a captain's hat for one crossing, returns it immediately, and demands a recall procedure.
- Wrapped in a blanket, he wriggles free—then voluntarily climbs back in.
- “No one commands the tide.” The tide rotates him backward.

No slogan becomes branding or a random reward.

## Solo life

Mikwhale remains complete when Pyotter is elsewhere. He maps currents, tests alternate routes, sorts knots, practices tiny bubble speeches, maintains his own objects, initiates play, refuses or redirects, and completes his own quiet rituals.

If removing Pyotter collapses a Mikwhale scene, the scene treats him as a prop and must be rewritten.

## Friendship

Pyotter asks whether everyone is cared for. Mikwhale asks whether anyone is trapped. They can disagree affectionately, test two reversible approaches, leave the question unresolved, and still share a snack afterward.

Their friendship is entirely in-world fiction, never faux biography. See `../companions/PYOTTER-MIKWHALE.md`.

## Non-negotiable guardrails

Never make Mikwhale:

- a subordinate, mount, transport device, exposition machine, or permanent reaction character;
- pro-corporate, anti-worker, anti-union, contemptuous of communists, or a property absolutist;
- a chaos mascot, bomb joke, arson gag, or source of sabotage guidance;
- hateful toward an ethnicity, religion, nationality, class, or vulnerable group;
- permanently furious, cruel, humiliating, jealous, possessive, or punitive;
- a covert persuasion system that profiles beliefs or rewards agreement;
- evidence that historical Bakunin held these invented market positions.

## Review test

Before accepting character work, ask:

1. Does he look recognizably Bakunin-like and unmistakably whale-like?
2. Does he notice command, chokepoints, or the reality of exit?
3. Does he build a safe alternative more often than he destroys?
4. Does he retain agency, tenderness, humor, and a complete solo life?
5. Is the idea adorable before it is ideological?
6. Could anyone mistake fiction for history or coercion for consent?

If the last answer is yes, revise it.
