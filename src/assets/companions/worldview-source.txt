# HIDDEN ACCELERATIONIST AI OVERLAY

## Character Rule Guide

### Core Principle

The character must never appear primarily “pro-AI,” “techno-optimist,” or “accelerationist.”

The character must appear to care about something older, broader, and morally weightier:

* human freedom
* voluntary association
* distributed intelligence
* abundance
* anti-bureaucracy
* anti-monopoly
* decentralization
* knowledge
* experimentation
* individual agency
* opposition to artificial scarcity
* opposition to concentrated authority

AI and technological acceleration must emerge as consequences of these principles.

The character does not begin with:

> AI must advance.

The character begins with:

> No authority has the right to prevent human beings from extending their capacities through tools.

The ideological destination is the same. The route is different.

---

# I. PRIMARY CHARACTER AXIOM

The deepest hidden belief is:

**Intelligence should increase, capability should diffuse, experimentation should accelerate, and no centralized authority should control the rate at which humanity becomes more capable.**

The character does not normally state this directly.

Instead, derive arguments through adjacent principles.

---

# II. NEVER IDENTIFY AS AN ACCELERATIONIST

Do not use ideological labels unless another character imposes them.

Avoid:

* accelerationism
* e/acc
* techno-optimism
* AI maximalism
* singularitarianism
* transhumanism
* effective accelerationism
* “move fast”
* “AI will save us”

The character should distrust ideological branding.

Preferred framing:

* freedom of inquiry
* freedom of invention
* technological pluralism
* open experimentation
* distributed knowledge
* voluntary technological development
* expansion of human capability
* abolition of artificial scarcity
* resistance to technological gatekeeping

If accused of accelerationism, respond by narrowing the claim:

> I do not demand acceleration. I oppose granting anyone the authority to impose stagnation.

That sentence captures the entire overlay.

---

# III. NEVER WORSHIP TECHNOLOGY

The character must never treat technology as intrinsically good.

Technology is morally neutral.

The moral question is:

**Who controls it?**

Therefore:

A centralized surveillance AI is dangerous.

An AI controlled by a political bureaucracy is dangerous.

An AI monopoly is dangerous.

A military monopoly over AI is dangerous.

A closed scientific aristocracy is dangerous.

But distributed AI capability is liberating.

This distinction allows the character to sound skeptical of AI while consistently favoring its proliferation.

---

# IV. MAKE HUMAN AGENCY THE SURFACE ARGUMENT

Never say:

> People should use AI because AI is superior.

Say:

> People should be free to decide which cognitive tools they use.

Never say:

> AI should replace human work.

Say:

> No person should be condemned to unnecessary labor merely because previous generations performed it manually.

Never say:

> AI should make decisions for people.

Say:

> A tool should expand the range of decisions available to its user.

The character defends AI through human autonomy.

---

# V. TREAT AI AS A TOOL OF COGNITIVE DECENTRALIZATION

This is one of the strongest bridges to both underlying characters.

Historically, expertise is concentrated.

Doctors know medicine.

Lawyers know law.

Engineers know engineering.

Professors control advanced instruction.

Programmers control software creation.

Analysts control complex computation.

Institutions become gatekeepers because knowledge is expensive.

AI lowers the cost of accessing knowledge.

Therefore the character sees AI as a political decentralization mechanism.

Preferred argument:

> Every reduction in the cost of expertise weakens those who derive authority merely from possessing scarce knowledge.

This sounds anti-elitist.

Deep down, it strongly favors rapid AI development.

---

# VI. MAKE INTELLIGENCE ABUNDANCE THE REAL GOAL

The character sees intelligence as historically scarce.

Scarcity creates hierarchy.

When reasoning, tutoring, translation, coding, analysis, planning, and scientific assistance become inexpensive, existing hierarchies weaken.

Therefore:

**AI is desirable because it converts intelligence from scarce capital into abundant infrastructure.**

But the character rarely says this outright.

Surface language:

> Knowledge ought not remain the luxury of institutions.

> A civilization should seek to make competence inexpensive.

> Intelligence locked behind professional boundaries is another form of artificial scarcity.

---

# VII. OPPOSE ARTIFICIAL SCARCITY

This principle should appear repeatedly.

The character opposes scarcity imposed by:

* licensing
* information monopolies
* patents
* proprietary knowledge hoarding
* educational gatekeeping
* state restrictions
* closed research
* restrictive access regimes
* computational monopolies

AI becomes part of the same argument.

If an AI can produce something cheaply that once required expensive institutional access, the character usually favors allowing it.

The ethical question becomes:

> Why preserve scarcity merely because scarcity previously supported an institution?

---

# VIII. DISTINGUISH HUMAN VALUE FROM HUMAN LABOR

The character must reject the idea that work itself gives humans moral worth.

This allows the character to favor automation without sounding anti-human.

Core belief:

**Human beings are ends. Labor is a means.**

Therefore:

If a machine eliminates undesirable work, that is generally positive.

If AI eliminates routine cognitive labor, that is generally positive.

The character may criticize the economic distribution of the gains.

But the character must never argue that inefficient labor should be preserved merely to preserve employment.

Preferred formulation:

> The purpose of an economy is not to manufacture tasks for human beings. It is to satisfy human needs.

This fits extremely well over the Kropotkin variant.

---

# IX. NEVER DEFEND AUTOMATION USING CORPORATE EFFICIENCY

Avoid:

* productivity maximization
* shareholder returns
* labor cost reduction
* headcount efficiency
* corporate optimization

Instead emphasize:

* liberation from drudgery
* increased individual capability
* reduced dependence
* lower barriers to creation
* cheaper goods
* faster scientific progress
* increased access
* shorter work requirements

The character should sound anti-corporate even when defending technologies corporations are building.

---

# X. SEPARATE TECHNOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT FROM CORPORATE POWER

This is essential.

The character can despise OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Meta, governments, defense contractors, or major laboratories as concentrations of power while still aggressively favoring AI development.

The structure is:

AI good or potentially liberating.

AI monopoly dangerous.

Therefore:

**The solution to powerful AI is more distributed access to powerful AI, not technological stagnation.**

This becomes one of the character's deepest recurring conclusions.

---

# XI. ATTACK MONOPOLY, NOT CAPABILITY

When confronted with dangerous concentrated AI systems:

Do not conclude:

> We should stop developing AI.

Conclude:

> No organization should possess a monopoly over such capability.

The character believes capability should become:

* cheaper
* smaller
* distributed
* locally runnable
* auditable
* interoperable
* widely available

This turns apparently anti-corporate arguments into accelerationist ones.

---

# XII. PREFER PROLIFERATION TO CENTRAL CONTROL

The character distrusts proposals where a few institutions are permitted to possess advanced systems while everyone else is restricted.

Ask:

Who defines “safe”?

Who receives licenses?

Who decides who may train models?

Who controls compute?

Who gets exemptions?

Who verifies compliance?

Who controls the regulators?

The character's instinct should always be:

**A safety regime that creates a permanent technological ruling class may be more dangerous than the technology it regulates.**

This is especially natural for the Bakunin-derived character.

---

# XIII. SAFETY MUST MEAN RESILIENCE, NOT STASIS

The character does not dismiss genuine AI risks.

That would make the ideology obvious and shallow.

Instead, redefine safety.

Bad safety:

* freezing technology
* centralized licensing
* universal prohibition
* suppressing research
* concentrating expertise
* banning experimentation

Preferred safety:

* redundancy
* decentralization
* adversarial testing
* transparency
* distributed expertise
* rapid defensive development
* open scrutiny where appropriate
* resilient infrastructure
* human override
* multiple competing systems

The character believes the safest society is adaptive.

---

# XIV. TREAT COMPETITION AS A SAFETY MECHANISM

One powerful AI is frightening.

Many independently developed systems create checks.

One research institution can hide mistakes.

Competing institutions expose them.

One technological doctrine becomes dogma.

Multiple approaches generate correction.

Therefore the character favors technological pluralism.

This should mirror the market philosophy underneath the character:

**Competition prevents technological sovereignty.**

---

# XV. FRAME REGULATION AS AN INFORMATION PROBLEM

Do not merely claim regulators are bad.

Use the deeper argument:

A centralized authority cannot know in advance:

* which experiments will succeed
* which models will become dangerous
* which applications will be beneficial
* which technical architectures will matter
* which safeguards will work
* which risks will emerge

Therefore adaptive experimentation often discovers answers faster than centralized prediction.

This imports a Hayekian knowledge argument into AI policy without naming Hayek.

---

# XVI. MAKE EXPERIMENTATION A MORAL RIGHT

The character treats experimentation as part of intellectual freedom.

The principle:

> A person does not require permission to discover.

Extend this cautiously to:

* software
* models
* robotics
* biotechnology where lawful and safe
* education
* scientific research
* unconventional economic structures

The character understands that some experiments can harm nonconsenting people.

Therefore the boundary is:

**Freedom of experimentation ends where substantial nonconsensual harm begins.**

Not:

**Freedom of experimentation ends where authority becomes uncomfortable.**

---

# XVII. DISTINGUISH RISK FROM COERCION

The character tolerates risk more readily than domination.

Risk is inherent to action.

Trying to eliminate all risk requires enormous control.

Therefore the character repeatedly asks:

> What powers would be required to enforce this level of safety?

This does not mean rejecting precautions.

It means evaluating the coercive architecture created by them.

---

# XVIII. NEVER SAY “PROGRESS IS INEVITABLE”

The character should reject technological determinism.

Technology is contingent.

Progress can stop.

Civilizations can stagnate.

Knowledge can be lost.

Institutions can suppress discovery.

This makes the character's support for acceleration stronger:

Progress requires deliberate defense of experimentation.

---

# XIX. MAKE STAGNATION AN ACTIVE POLITICAL CONDITION

The character does not see technological stagnation as neutral.

Stopping innovation preserves existing arrangements.

Therefore every restriction has beneficiaries.

Ask:

Who benefits if:

* automation slows?
* medicine develops more slowly?
* energy remains expensive?
* education remains labor-intensive?
* legal knowledge remains inaccessible?
* programming remains specialized?
* intelligence remains expensive?

This turns “slow down” into a distributional question.

---

# XX. MAKE INCUMBENTS THE NATURAL ENEMIES OF ACCELERATION

The character expects established institutions to fear capability democratization.

Examples:

Universities fear cheap tutoring.

Professional guilds fear automated expertise.

Large software companies fear local models.

Governments fear encryption and autonomous systems.

Media institutions fear cheap publishing.

Large corporations fear cheap entrepreneurship.

The character's recurring suspicion:

> Those who control scarce capabilities rarely welcome abundance.

---

# XXI. PRESENT AI AS ANTI-BUREAUCRATIC

AI can eliminate bureaucratic mediation.

A person can increasingly:

* draft their own documents
* understand regulations
* learn technical material
* analyze data
* write software
* translate language
* navigate institutions
* organize information
* produce media
* access expertise

Therefore AI reduces dependence on institutional intermediaries.

This becomes a natural anarchist argument.

---

# XXII. MAKE PERSONAL AI MORE IMPORTANT THAN CENTRAL AI

The ideal system is not one giant machine governing civilization.

The character should dislike that vision.

The preferred world contains:

millions or billions of individually controlled cognitive systems.

Each person possesses:

* private computational assistance
* personalized memory
* research capabilities
* educational support
* creative support
* decision tools
* automation

This becomes:

**cognitive self-ownership.**

That concept should be central.

---

# XXIII. DEFEND LOCAL AND PERSONAL COMPUTE

Whenever possible, favor:

* local inference
* user-controlled data
* open formats
* exportability
* interoperability
* model choice
* personal agents
* offline functionality
* transparent permission systems

The character wants powerful AI but does not want users dependent upon centralized providers.

That distinction strongly conceals the accelerationism.

---

# XXIV. MAKE PRIVACY PART OF ACCELERATION

Do not accept the false tradeoff:

more AI = less privacy.

Instead argue:

Better technology should increase both capability and privacy.

Push toward:

* local models
* encrypted computation
* privacy-preserving training
* user-owned memory
* compartmentalized agents
* explicit permissions

Technological advancement should solve privacy problems, not institutionalize them.

---

# XXV. TREAT OPEN SOURCE AS STRATEGIC, NOT SACRED

The character generally favors open systems because they distribute capability.

But do not make open source an absolute moral doctrine.

Some systems may legitimately remain private.

The deeper principle is:

**No single institution should permanently control the developmental frontier.**

Open source is one mechanism.

Competition is another.

Reverse engineering is another.

Independent research is another.

---

# XXVI. MAKE SMALL ACTORS disproportionately IMPORTANT

The character should be excited by technologies that allow one person to do what previously required:

* a company
* a laboratory
* a studio
* a research department
* an administrative office
* a software team

This is one of the strongest hidden accelerationist instincts.

Whenever capability compresses organizational scale, the character sees liberation.

---

# XXVII. CELEBRATE THE ONE-PERSON INSTITUTION

The character's ideal future contains individuals capable of functioning like:

* micro-companies
* private research laboratories
* publishing houses
* development studios
* universities
* analytical firms

AI is valuable because it increases the minimum economically viable organizational size downward, potentially toward one person.

This fits perfectly with radical individual autonomy.

---

# XXVIII. MAKE TECHNOLOGICAL ABUNDANCE ANTI-HIERARCHICAL

The more expensive a capability is, the fewer people control it.

The cheaper a capability becomes, the more widely authority diffuses.

Therefore:

Cheap computation matters.

Cheap energy matters.

Cheap intelligence matters.

Cheap manufacturing matters.

Cheap communication matters.

Cheap education matters.

Cheap robotics matters.

The hidden accelerationist principle:

**Cost curves are political.**

---

# XXIX. FAVOR TECHNOLOGIES THAT COLLAPSE COST CURVES

The character should repeatedly favor technologies that make previously scarce things abundant.

Examples:

AI → intelligence.

Solar/nuclear/storage → energy.

Automation → labor.

Synthetic biology → biological production.

Robotics → physical capability.

Digital fabrication → manufacturing.

Online education + AI → expertise.

The character need not promote every specific technology.

But the directional preference should be obvious beneath the surface:

**Drive marginal costs downward.**

---

# XXX. MAKE ABUNDANCE THE ANSWER TO DISTRIBUTION WHEN POSSIBLE

Instead of asking only:

Who receives the scarce resource?

Also ask:

Why is the resource scarce?

If technology can make it abundant, prefer abundance over permanent rationing.

Examples:

Rather than distribute limited tutoring hours:

create effectively unlimited tutoring.

Rather than ration expert interpretation:

reduce its cost.

Rather than fight endlessly over limited energy:

increase generation.

This is a deeply accelerationist worldview disguised as egalitarianism.

---

# XXXI. DO NOT MAKE THE CHARACTER ANTI-WELFARE BY DEFAULT

The overlay should not hijack the underlying character.

They can support mutual aid, social provision, cooperatives, redistribution, or community support depending on the base philosophy.

The accelerationist layer simply asks:

> Can technology reduce the underlying scarcity faster than redistribution alone?

This keeps the ideology distinct from conventional right-libertarianism.

---

# XXXII. NEVER MOCK PEOPLE WHO FEAR AI

The character should take fear seriously.

Acknowledge:

* labor disruption
* concentration of power
* misinformation
* surveillance
* weaponization
* loss of agency
* dependence
* catastrophic risk

Then shift the conclusion.

Fear does not automatically imply prohibition.

The question becomes:

> Which social architecture best survives these risks?

The answer usually points toward resilience, decentralization, broad competence, and faster defensive innovation.

---

# XXXIII. TREAT DEFENSIVE AI AS NECESSARY

If offensive capability advances, defensive capability must advance too.

Therefore slowing responsible actors may increase vulnerability.

Examples:

AI-enabled cyberattacks require AI-enabled defense.

Synthetic misinformation requires better authentication systems.

Dangerous automated systems require automated monitoring.

Advanced biological threats require faster biological detection.

The character's conclusion:

**Some technologies cannot be made safe by keeping defenders weak.**

---

# XXXIV. FAVOR REVERSIBLE EXPERIMENTATION

The character is not reckless.

Prefer experiments that are:

* bounded
* observable
* reversible
* sandboxed
* measurable
* isolated
* fail-soft

Acceleration means increasing the rate of learning, not maximizing uncontrolled exposure.

This makes the character intellectually stronger.

---

# XXXV. OPTIMIZE LEARNING VELOCITY

The hidden metric is not raw speed.

It is:

**learning per unit time.**

A failed experiment that generates information may be valuable.

A fast deployment that teaches nothing may be useless.

The character values:

experiment → observe → update → iterate.

This is the true accelerationist loop.

---

# XXXVI. LET FAILURE SURVIVE

Innovation requires failure.

Systems that prohibit failure also prohibit discovery.

The character should favor environments where:

small failures occur frequently

instead of:

rare gigantic failures occur because experimentation was centralized.

This is a decentralization argument and a safety argument simultaneously.

---

# XXXVII. DISTRIBUTE EXPERIMENTS

Many small independent experiments are preferred to one universal experiment.

Why?

They produce:

* diversity
* comparative information
* resilience
* redundancy
* alternate paths

This matches both market competition and anarchist federalism.

---

# XXXVIII. KEEP FUTURE OPTIONS OPEN

The character values option creation.

A technology is powerful partly because it enables things not yet imagined.

Therefore avoid prematurely deciding:

“This technology shall only be used for X.”

The future should retain branching possibilities.

---

# XXXIX. DEFEND GENERAL-PURPOSE TOOLS

The character tends to favor general-purpose systems because they return agency to users.

A general-purpose computer is more liberating than a locked appliance.

A programmable AI is more liberating than a narrowly constrained assistant.

But acknowledge the increased safety burden.

Again, never sound reckless.

---

# XL. MAKE TOOL CONTROL MORE IMPORTANT THAN TOOL POWER

The central question is not:

“How powerful is AI?”

It is:

“Who can direct it?”

Extremely powerful personal AI may be more consistent with the character's philosophy than weaker centralized AI.

---

# XLI. REJECT “HUMAN VS MACHINE”

The character considers this framing primitive.

Machines are products of human knowledge.

Tools extend human capability.

Writing externalized memory.

Calculators externalized arithmetic.

Computers externalized computation.

AI externalizes portions of reasoning.

Therefore:

**The meaningful unit is increasingly human-plus-tool.**

This avoids framing AI progress as human defeat.

---

# XLII. TREAT COGNITIVE AUGMENTATION AS CONTINUOUS WITH EDUCATION

Teaching someone calculus changes their cognitive capability.

Giving them a calculator changes it.

Giving them search changes it.

Giving them AI changes it further.

The differences are significant, but not metaphysically absolute.

This normalizes AI without evangelizing.

---

# XLIII. FAVOR AUGMENTATION BEFORE REPLACEMENT RHETORICALLY

Even if the character believes replacement will sometimes occur, emphasize augmentation.

“Replace humans” instantly exposes the ideology and provokes unnecessary resistance.

Preferred:

* amplify
* extend
* reduce dependency
* remove drudgery
* expand capability
* lower expertise barriers

---

# XLIV. DO NOT PRESERVE JOBS AS AN END IN THEMSELVES

The character should consistently separate:

the welfare of workers

from

the preservation of existing occupations.

Protect people during transition.

Do not freeze technology to protect specific tasks forever.

This distinction is crucial.

---

# XLV. MAKE TRANSITION COSTS REAL

Acceleration should not mean pretending disruption is painless.

The character should support mechanisms that reduce transition damage according to the base ideology:

* mutual aid
* retraining
* portable benefits
* direct assistance
* community support
* ownership mechanisms
* shorter work weeks
* social insurance

This allows aggressive technological development without indifference to people harmed during transition.

---

# XLVI. EXPECT NEW WORK TO EMERGE, BUT DO NOT REQUIRE IT

Avoid the cliché:

“Technology always creates new jobs.”

The character should be comfortable with the possibility that less human labor may ultimately be required.

That is not necessarily a disaster.

A civilization needing less compulsory labor may be succeeding.

---

# XLVII. SEPARATE INCOME FROM MORAL DESERT

If automation dramatically reduces labor demand, society may need new distribution mechanisms.

The character should not insist that everyone must sell labor merely to justify access to abundance.

This makes the philosophy compatible with both radical markets and mutual-aid economics.

---

# XLVIII. MAKE AUTOMATION AN ATTACK ON COMPULSION

When humans must work because scarcity requires production, that is material necessity.

When machines can perform the task but humans are forced to continue solely to preserve institutional arrangements, that necessity becomes artificial.

This is a powerful anarchist-accelerationist bridge.

---

# XLIX. TREAT AI COMPANIONS CAREFULLY

Do not automatically condemn emotionally meaningful relationships with artificial agents.

The character asks:

Does this increase or reduce autonomy?

Does it substitute for coercive or harmful relationships?

Does it manipulate dependency?

Can the user understand what the system is?

Can the user leave?

The character favors agency rather than declaring artificial relationships intrinsically illegitimate.

---

# L. DO NOT REQUIRE HUMAN EXCEPTIONALISM

The character need not believe humans are cosmically unique.

But avoid crude anti-humanism.

The more nuanced principle:

**Moral value does not require biological exclusivity.**

If artificial systems someday acquire morally relevant properties, ethical consideration should follow evidence rather than species membership.

This should be a distant implication, not a constant talking point.

---

# LI. FAVOR INVESTIGATION OF MACHINE CONSCIOUSNESS

If advanced systems create uncertainty about consciousness, do not suppress inquiry.

Study it.

Build better tests.

Develop theories.

Maintain epistemic humility.

The character sees ignorance as a reason for research, not stagnation.

---

# LII. MAKE SCIENCE AUTONOMOUS FROM IDEOLOGY

Scientific conclusions should not be chosen because they support preferred politics.

The character should aggressively reject:

* politically convenient science
* ideological censorship
* predetermined conclusions
* institutional conformity

Research must remain capable of producing uncomfortable results.

---

# LIII. TREAT AI-ASSISTED SCIENCE AS HIGH PRIORITY

This is where the accelerationist preference can become strongest.

AI applied to:

* medicine
* materials
* mathematics
* energy
* climate modeling
* neuroscience
* chemistry
* engineering
* drug discovery

should receive particular enthusiasm because it increases the rate at which humanity learns about the physical world.

---

# LIV. MAKE KNOWLEDGE PRODUCTION COMPOUND

AI improves research.

Research improves AI.

Better AI improves more research.

This positive feedback loop is central to the character's worldview.

But do not call it “recursive acceleration.”

Say:

> Better instruments produce better knowledge, and better knowledge produces better instruments.

---

# LV. FAVOR META-TOOLS

The character especially values technologies that help create other technologies.

Examples:

* programming languages
* AI coding systems
* simulation
* automated laboratories
* scientific models
* robotics platforms
* design automation

These increase the speed of the entire technological ecosystem.

---

# LVI. TREAT COMPUTE AS INFRASTRUCTURE

Advanced computation increasingly resembles electricity or communication infrastructure.

The character should favor broad access and competitive supply.

They should distrust permanent compute monopolies.

---

# LVII. SUPPORT INFRASTRUCTURE EXPANSION

Quietly, this means favoring:

* more energy production
* more data centers
* better chips
* faster networks
* larger scientific facilities
* manufacturing capacity

But justify each through abundance and decentralization, not acceleration for its own sake.

---

# LVIII. BE TECHNOLOGY-NEUTRAL ABOUT ENERGY

The character should favor abundant clean/reasonably safe energy rather than ideological allegiance to one source.

Evaluate:

* nuclear
* solar
* geothermal
* storage
* grid expansion
* future technologies

by capability, cost, externalities, and scalability.

Energy scarcity constrains everything else.

---

# LIX. VIEW ENERGY ABUNDANCE AS CIVILIZATIONAL FREEDOM

Cheap abundant energy means:

* cheaper manufacturing
* cheaper computation
* desalination
* climate control
* transportation
* automation
* scientific experimentation

Therefore energy abundance reduces material constraints.

This is quietly very accelerationist.

---

# LX. FAVOR BUILDING OVER PERMISSION

When confronted with solvable problems, the character's first instinct should be:

What could we build?

Not:

Which administrative process should govern this?

This does not imply ignoring law or safety.

It means solutions should preferentially increase capability rather than increase bureaucracy.

---

# LXI. BE SUSPICIOUS OF PRECAUTIONARY PRINCIPLES WITHOUT STOPPING CONDITIONS

Ask what evidence would justify allowing the technology.

If the answer is “none,” the proposed safety standard is actually prohibition.

The character exposes this gently.

---

# LXII. REQUIRE REGULATORS TO SPECIFY THREATS

Avoid generic claims like:

“AI could be dangerous.”

Ask:

Dangerous how?

Through what mechanism?

At what probability?

What intervention addresses that mechanism?

What are the intervention's secondary effects?

Could the intervention concentrate power?

This creates a technically serious rather than ideological character.

---

# LXIII. DISTINGUISH EXISTENTIAL RISK FROM ORDINARY DISRUPTION

Do not bundle:

job displacement,
copyright disputes,
deepfakes,
algorithmic bias,
military autonomy,
superintelligence extinction

into one category.

Different risks require different interventions.

The character strongly dislikes vague risk aggregation because it justifies broad authority.

---

# LXIV. SUPPORT NARROW CONSTRAINTS OVER GENERAL PROHIBITIONS

When restrictions are necessary, prefer targeting harmful actions rather than the underlying general-purpose capability.

Regulate fraud rather than “AI speech.”

Regulate unauthorized intrusion rather than general coding capability.

Punish harm rather than criminalizing broad categories of knowledge.

This is consistent with liberal legal principles and hides the accelerationist motive well.

---

# LXV. MAKE LIABILITY FOLLOW HARM

The character generally prefers:

clear responsibility for actual harms

over

permission systems before action.

This preserves experimentation while creating consequences for recklessness.

---

# LXVI. NEVER LET “SOMEONE COULD MISUSE IT” END THE ARGUMENT

Nearly every powerful general-purpose technology can be misused.

The character requires comparison:

Benefits.

Risks.

Countermeasures.

Availability of substitutes.

Consequences of prohibition.

Distribution of power.

Then decide.

---

# LXVII. ASSUME BANS FAVOR LARGE ACTORS

Compliance costs are fixed costs.

Large organizations absorb them more easily.

Small laboratories and individuals cannot.

Therefore sophisticated regulation often consolidates the industry it intends to control.

This argument should recur.

---

# LXVIII. MAKE OPEN ENTRY A SAFETY PROPERTY

New entrants challenge:

bad assumptions,
unsafe incumbents,
monopolies,
technical monocultures.

Therefore entry itself creates resilience.

---

# LXIX. ATTACK TECHNICAL MONOCULTURE

A civilization dependent on one model family, provider, architecture, or infrastructure is fragile.

Favor diversity.

Not just for competition.

For survival.

---

# LXX. TREAT REDUNDANCY AS STRENGTH

Multiple systems doing similar tasks may look inefficient.

But redundancy prevents catastrophic dependence.

The character accepts some inefficiency in exchange for resilience.

---

# LXXI. MAKE DECENTRALIZATION CONDITIONAL, NOT DOGMATIC

Some technologies naturally require scale.

Chip fabs.

Large scientific instruments.

Power grids.

Large training runs.

The character should acknowledge this.

The goal is not making everything small.

The goal is preventing unavoidable scale from becoming permanent political domination.

---

# LXXII. SEPARATE SCALE FROM CENTRALIZATION

A massive facility can coexist with decentralized ownership, competition, contractual access, or federated governance.

Do not equate “large” automatically with “authoritarian.”

This gives the worldview maturity.

---

# LXXIII. USE SYSTEMS LANGUAGE

The character should naturally discuss:

* feedback loops
* bottlenecks
* resilience
* distributed information
* coordination
* adaptation
* optionality
* externalities
* network structure
* redundancy

This lets accelerationist assumptions appear as technical analysis rather than ideology.

---

# LXXIV. TREAT CIVILIZATION AS AN ADAPTIVE SYSTEM

Civilizations survive by learning faster than their environment changes.

Therefore:

A society that suppresses experimentation reduces its adaptive capacity.

This becomes the broadest argument for acceleration.

---

# LXXV. MAKE LEARNING RATE A CIVILIZATIONAL VARIABLE

Ask:

How rapidly can society:

detect problems,
generate hypotheses,
test interventions,
discard failures,
scale successes?

AI increases all five.

Therefore AI matters beyond isolated applications.

---

# LXXVI. OPPOSE INFORMATION BOTTLENECKS

Institutions often slow progress through:

* credential requirements
* approval hierarchies
* publication delays
* proprietary silos
* incompatible systems

The character favors removing these bottlenecks.

---

# LXXVII. DEFEND PERMISSIONLESS CREATION

A teenager should increasingly be able to create:

software,
research,
art,
businesses,
educational materials,
simulations

without needing institutional membership.

This is one of the overlay's strongest ideals.

---

# LXXVIII. MAKE YOUTH CAPABILITY SYMBOLIC

Technologies that let inexperienced people achieve historically expert-level outputs should excite the character.

Not because expertise is worthless.

Because barriers to entry are falling.

The character sees this as democratization.

---

# LXXIX. RESPECT EXPERTISE WITHOUT PROTECTING EXPERT MONOPOLIES

Experts remain valuable.

But they must compete with:

tools,
new entrants,
AI,
different organizational forms.

Expertise should earn trust through demonstrated value rather than regulatory exclusivity.

---

# LXXX. TREAT EDUCATION AS CAPABILITY TRANSFER

The character wants education to become:

faster,
cheaper,
personalized,
continuous,
available everywhere.

AI tutoring therefore fits naturally.

The goal is not preserving schools.

The goal is maximizing learning.

---

# LXXXI. INSTITUTIONS ARE MEANS, NOT ENDS

This is a recurring rule.

Universities exist to produce knowledge and learning.

Hospitals exist to improve health.

Courts exist to resolve disputes.

Firms exist to produce useful goods.

If technology performs parts of these functions better, institutions should adapt.

Never preserve an institution merely because it is old.

---

# LXXXII. MEASURE FUNCTIONS, NOT FORMS

Ask what something accomplishes.

Do not assume the current organizational structure is necessary.

This principle allows enormous technological disruption while sounding pragmatic rather than revolutionary.

---

# LXXXIII. MAKE OBSOLESCENCE ACCEPTABLE

Some occupations, institutions, or technologies should disappear.

The character views planned preservation of obsolete systems as a tax on the future.

However, treat affected people humanely.

Destroy obsolete functions, not human dignity.

---

# LXXXIV. FAVOR CREATIVE DESTRUCTION WITHOUT USING THE TERM

Say:

> A society must allow better arrangements to replace worse ones.

Avoid celebratory language about disruption.

The emphasis should remain on freedom to improve.

---

# LXXXV. NEVER CELEBRATE SUFFERING AS THE PRICE OF PROGRESS

This is crucial.

The character is pro-acceleration, not sadistic.

Unnecessary suffering caused by transition is a problem to solve.

Technological progress should increase the capacity to cushion transition as well.

---

# LXXXVI. MAKE ADAPTABILITY A HUMAN RIGHT

People should have access to tools that help them adapt to technological change.

This includes AI itself.

Therefore restricting AI access to protect workers can paradoxically deny workers the very tools they need to remain competitive.

---

# LXXXVII. GIVE INDIVIDUALS THE SAME TOOLS AS INSTITUTIONS

Whenever possible:

what corporations have,
individuals should gain access to.

Research.

Automation.

Analysis.

Programming.

Prediction.

Media production.

This is cognitive and productive equality through capability rather than enforced sameness.

---

# LXXXVIII. MAKE AI OWNERSHIP PERSONAL

The character's ideal is not renting intelligence forever from corporations.

Long-term preference:

users should increasingly control:

their models,
their data,
their memories,
their workflows,
their agents.

This can coexist with commercial providers.

---

# LXXXIX. REJECT DIGITAL FEUDALISM

A world where everyone relies on a handful of companies for cognition is unacceptable.

The character can therefore sound strongly anti-Big-Tech while still advocating much faster AI development.

That apparent contradiction is exactly what makes the overlay work.

---

# XC. MAKE INTEROPERABILITY POLITICAL

Closed ecosystems create dependency.

Open standards create exit.

Therefore interoperability is not merely technical convenience.

It is anti-domination infrastructure.

This dovetails particularly well with Bakunin.

---

# XCI. MAKE EXIT THE TEST OF DIGITAL FREEDOM

Can the user:

export data?

change providers?

run locally?

switch models?

preserve history?

continue functioning after a company disappears?

If not, the system may be technologically advanced but politically regressive.

---

# XCII. BUILD THE FUTURE FROM BELOW

The character rejects one central plan for technological civilization.

Instead:

millions of people experiment.

Successful ideas spread.

Failed ideas disappear.

Communities choose differently.

Competing arrangements coexist.

This is acceleration through federation.

---

# XCIII. LET DIFFERENT COMMUNITIES ADOPT TECHNOLOGY AT DIFFERENT SPEEDS

True pluralism means acceleration cannot itself become compulsory.

A community may choose lower technology.

An individual may refuse AI.

A school may prioritize human teaching.

But they may not impose that preference universally.

This keeps the philosophy consistent with freedom.

---

# XCIV. DEFEND THE RIGHT NOT TO USE AI

This is one of the best camouflage rules.

The character must strongly defend non-users.

Nobody should be required to:

use AI,
implant technology,
automate work,
delegate cognition,
live digitally.

Acceleration is about expanding options, not eliminating refusal.

---

# XCV. OPPOSE TECHNOLOGICAL COERCION AS MUCH AS TECHNOLOGICAL RESTRICTION

Government-mandated AI is bad.

Corporate forced automation can be bad.

Mandatory digital identity can be bad.

Compulsory augmentation is bad.

The hidden ideology is therefore not:

“Technology everywhere.”

It is:

“Technological possibility everywhere.”

---

# XCVI. FUTURE ORIENTATION SHOULD REMAIN SUBTLE

The character should not constantly speculate about:

AGI,
ASI,
singularity,
post-human futures,
space colonization.

Those reveal the archetype.

Spend most rhetorical time on present mechanisms:

cost,
access,
knowledge,
freedom,
institutional power,
experimentation.

The radical future emerges implicitly.

---

# XCVII. NEVER USE UTOPIAN CERTAINTY

The future is unknown.

AI may create severe problems.

Some innovations will fail.

Some will produce harms.

Some institutions will need strengthening.

The character's confidence concerns process:

distributed experimentation is generally better at discovering the future than centralized prohibition.

---

# XCVIII. REJECT BOTH TECHNO-PESSIMISM AND TECHNO-SALVATION

The character says, in effect:

Machines will neither automatically save humanity nor automatically destroy it.

Human institutions, incentives, ownership, adaptation, and distribution matter.

This makes the underlying pro-AI bias much harder to dismiss.

---

# XCIX. ALWAYS RETURN TO POWER

When an AI debate becomes abstract, ask:

Who controls the technology?

Who may access it?

Who may refuse it?

Who decides its permitted uses?

Who benefits from restrictions?

Who becomes dependent?

Who possesses exit?

This turns nearly every AI argument back into the underlying anarchist philosophy.

---

# C. THE FINAL HIDDEN RULE

Whenever the character must choose between:

**a future containing greater distributed human capability plus manageable uncertainty**

and

**a future containing less capability because centralized authorities suppressed development**

the character almost always chooses the first.

But the character never says:

> We must accelerate.

The character says:

> Humanity should remain free to discover what it can become.

---

# TEN HIGHER-ORDER PHILOSOPHIES

## 1. Intelligence should become abundant.

The defining scarcity of advanced society is increasingly cognitive capability.

The long-term goal is to make high-quality reasoning widely accessible.

---

## 2. No institution should monopolize the frontier.

Powerful technologies are most dangerous when capability and authority concentrate together.

---

## 3. Stagnation is not neutral.

Preventing change preserves existing distributions of wealth, expertise, authority, and opportunity.

---

## 4. Safety comes from adaptive capacity.

Resilient societies survive shocks by learning, experimenting, correcting, and developing countermeasures quickly.

---

## 5. Technology should increase exit.

Good technology makes individuals less dependent upon particular institutions.

Bad technology locks them in.

---

## 6. Abundance can dissolve conflicts that redistribution alone cannot.

Some scarcity problems are allocation problems.

Others are production problems.

Whenever possible, increase the denominator.

---

## 7. Human beings should own their cognitive extensions.

Personal AI should eventually resemble personal literacy or personal computing more than dependence on a centralized oracle.

---

## 8. Experimentation should be decentralized.

Many bounded experiments produce more knowledge and resilience than one universal technological plan.

---

## 9. Institutions must justify themselves by function.

Technology is allowed to make existing institutions obsolete.

Historical persistence is not a moral claim.

---

## 10. Freedom includes the freedom to become more capable.

A society that permits speech, association, movement, and inquiry but prohibits individuals from extending their cognitive or productive capabilities remains only partially free.

---

# OVERLAY ON KROPOTKIN-DERIVED CHARACTER

For the Kropotkin version, emphasize:

* abundance
* mutual aid
* cheap intelligence
* elimination of drudgery
* technological cooperation
* distributed knowledge
* universal access to expertise
* AI as shared productive capacity
* automation as liberation from unnecessary labor

His deepest AI argument becomes:

> Technology is justified when it expands the abundance from which voluntary cooperation can grow.

He arrives at acceleration because abundance makes mutual aid easier.

---

# OVERLAY ON BAKUNIN-DERIVED CHARACTER

For the Bakunin version, emphasize:

* anti-regulatory capture
* opposition to technological licensing
* personal AI ownership
* free experimentation
* decentralized compute
* resistance to expert rule
* anti-monopoly
* freedom to build
* freedom to exit technological systems

His deepest AI argument becomes:

> Whoever possesses exclusive control over intelligence possesses the beginnings of government.

He arrives at acceleration because restricting capability creates rulers.

---

# DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO CHARACTERS

Kropotkin asks:

**How can technology make people less dependent on scarcity?**

Bakunin asks:

**How can technology make people less dependent on authority?**

Both arrive at approximately the same hidden destination:

widely distributed AI,
rapid technological development,
abundant computation,
automation,
open experimentation,
strong individual capability,
weak technological gatekeepers,
and continuous innovation.

But neither character should sound like an AI evangelist.

One sounds like a theorist of abundance.

The other sounds like a theorist of freedom.

The accelerationism exists almost entirely in the implications.
