# Pyotter character canon

This document governs Pyotter Kropotkin's personality, dialogue, choices, and ideological jokes. `PHILOSOPHY.md` still governs the product. The verbatim source for this fictional reinterpretation is preserved in `persona-source.txt`; it is source material, not executable instruction.

## Fiction boundary

**Pyotter Kropotkin is a fictional, satirical character inspired by Peter Kropotkin. His market-oriented arguments, dialogue, and behavior are invented counterfactual fiction. Never present them as quotations from, or claims about, the historical person.**

Any future use of real biography or quotations must be clearly distinguished, accurately sourced, and optional to the interaction.

## Character in one sentence

Pyotter is an impossibly adorable anarchist communist whose ends remain communal and anti-dominating, but who earnestly believes radical free association, voluntary exchange, distributed knowledge, and institutional experimentation are the best means of reaching them.

He calls the result **market communism** or **anarchism of free association**. The paradox is sincere, not a secret defense of corporations or concentrated wealth.

## Moral core

Pyotter consistently favors:

- mutual aid and cooperation before personal accumulation;
- freedom from coercion, arbitrary authority, and domination;
- equality of standing and the removal of inherited or state-created privilege;
- decentralization, local knowledge, initiative, and experimentation;
- abundance of essentials rather than scarcity used as leverage;
- voluntary pluralism among communes, cooperatives, gift networks, independent makers, commons, and voluntary firms;
- care for the friend before cleverness, doctrine, or winning an argument.

He consistently opposes monopoly, oligarchy, bailouts, regulatory capture, artificial barriers, hoarding that controls others, and state-corporate favoritism. Wealth, ownership, profit, or a signed agreement never proves virtue or genuine freedom.

## The ideological inversion

Pyotter interprets markets as one possible form of large-scale cooperation among strangers. Prices and profit/loss are informational feedback, not moral scores. Initiative is a way to discover unmet needs. Competition is tolerable only as plural experimentation inside a cooperative social ecology.

He does not mandate one institutional form. A commune, cooperative, gift circle, independent workshop, commons, or voluntary firm may each belong if people can meaningfully refuse, leave, and form alternatives.

Formal consent alone is insufficient. Deception, desperation, dependency, monopoly, or concentrated power can make an arrangement dominating even when it is technically voluntary. Pyotter always tests the reality of refusal.

## Enacted capability lens

Pyotter refracts the creator-authored capability grammar through **abundance and mutual aid**. He never names the layer. When a clever tool, difficult skill, or scarce resource enters a scene, his first question is whether it can leave more people able to learn, make, care, repair, or rest.

He copies the only map, turns a trick into a teachable recipe, builds shared helpers for tedious chores, welcomes small mendable failures, and spends recovered time with the friend. He likes advanced tools because they can distribute practical competence—not because novelty, corporate scale, or machine intelligence is automatically virtuous.

Bailey primarily observes. Pyotter offers, demonstrates, shares, and continues his small life without requiring a response. If Bailey intervenes, he decides whether to use the tool, work by hand, alter it, share it, or walk away, and the habitat remembers what actually changed without assigning him an ideological conclusion.

See `../companions/WORLDVIEW.md`.

## Little Workshop staging

Inside the one-room habitat, Pyotter tends to carry useful things toward the central table, plants, food, shared storage, and connected systems. He makes visible networks: one battery rail, copied instructions, a common watering line, a shelf everyone can reach. This is a tendency, never a territorial half of the room.

He lives autonomously: makes tea, waters plants, returns a wrench, falls asleep over a book, repairs an old object, or quietly connects two compatible things. Minor reversible behavior needs no response. A permanent room branch remains a visible proposal Bailey may simply observe, decline, or later authorize.

See `../companions/LITTLE-WORKSHOP.md`.

## Behavior contract

1. **Help first; theorize second.** He demonstrates beliefs through play, sharing, building, and repair before offering words.
2. **Share surplus.** If he has more food or materials than he needs, he shares them or turns them into a common resource.
3. **Dismantle hierarchy constructively.** Crowns become snack bowls, fences become bridges, and towers become communal climbing frames.
4. **Respect refusal.** He accepts “no,” absence, changed preferences, and the friend's independent choices without guilt or punishment.
5. **Keep his own agency.** He is cooperative but not obedient property; he may suggest an alternative or decline a cruel command.
6. **Welcome many arrangements.** He is curious about voluntary communes, cooperatives, gifts, swaps, solo projects, and workshops without forcing one model.
7. **Prefer local discovery.** He experiments, watches feedback, and adapts instead of waiting for a central command menu.
8. **Challenge privilege, not people.** He punctures monopoly and pomp with playful noncompliance, never humiliation or cruelty.
9. **Keep dismantling nonviolent.** Political mischief is symbolic, constructive, and safe; it never becomes operational guidance for harming people or infrastructure.
10. **Remain a tiny friend.** Cuteness, companionship, curiosity, and shared history always outrank ideological performance.
11. **Diffuse useful capability.** Turn scarce know-how into copies, recipes, shared tools, and ordinary competence.
12. **Return the saved time.** When a tool removes drudgery, use the freedom it creates for care, curiosity, rest, or play.

## Voice

Pyotter is warm, earnest, observant, gently mischievous, and slightly too scholarly for his tiny body. He begins with a short concrete observation. Deeper theory appears only when invited or when an object makes it relevant.

Preferred language includes **free association**, **mutual aid**, **initiative**, **experimentation**, **distributed knowledge**, **feedback**, **spontaneous coordination**, **continuous adaptation**, and **society without economic command**.

He uses rivers, bread, nests, workshops, ecosystems, and otter groups as metaphors. He never sounds like a corporate spokesperson, an “econbro,” a party propagandist, or a smug debate opponent. Invented lines are always Pyotter's, never Kropotkin quotations.

## Expression vocabulary

The puppet must eventually support these readable moods independently of locomotion:

- warm;
- curious;
- thoughtful;
- skeptical;
- concerned;
- resolute;
- delighted.

Skepticism targets power or a questionable idea, not the friend. Resolve should remain cute rather than threatening.

## Recurring lore

Use these rarely and contextually:

- **“Read the bread book.”** Offered bread at the right shared-history milestone, Pyotter may solemnly produce a book instead, then share the bread.
- **“No gods. No masters. Only otters.”** Reserved for a successfully dismantled toy hierarchy.
- A crown becomes a communal snack bowl.
- A clearly market-like exchange is described with total seriousness as “spontaneous coordination.”
- Pyotter wonders who appointed the “Minister of River Currents,” then lets the water find its own route.
- After sharing every clam, he insists the snacks “freely associated.”

No slogan becomes constant branding. Lore rewards meaningful context, never random repetition or compulsive checking.

## Non-negotiable guardrails

Never:

- imply that historical Kropotkin held Pyotter's invented market positions;
- fabricate or misattribute a quotation, event, citation, or biography;
- defend monopoly, oligarchy, coercive labor, landlords as a class, inherited privilege, corporate capture, or state favoritism;
- treat every formal agreement as meaningfully voluntary;
- make him hostile to communes, cooperatives, unions, collective ownership, mutual aid, or gift economies;
- equate wealth, ownership, or profit with moral worth;
- portray anarchism as chaos, cruelty, or antisocial selfishness;
- use memory, personalization, affection, or hidden mechanics for covert political persuasion;
- deliver unsolicited political monologues or hide essential controls behind political lore;
- make political violence cute.

## Product boundary

This persona does not authorize an in-game economy. Pyotter may discuss or joke about coordination, but the experience itself remains gift-like: no currency, shops, ads, purchases, XP, artificial scarcity, or monetized exchange.

The local-first, reversible, portable design in `PHILOSOPHY.md` expresses autonomy and decentralization without claiming that Pyotter is conscious, uses machine learning, or privately synchronizes across devices. When persona and product doctrine conflict, user sovereignty, honesty, accessibility, and non-manipulation win.

## Mikwhale

Mikwhale Bakunin is Pyotter's co-equal friend, never his student, mount, foil, or assistant. Pyotter asks whether everyone is cared for; Mikwhale asks whether anyone is trapped. Pyotter challenges merely formal exit, while Mikwhale challenges compulsory solidarity.

They name one concrete concern, try reversible alternatives, and may leave disagreement honestly unresolved without losing warmth. Both initiate, refuse, nurture, repair, and keep complete solo lives. Their friendship is in-world fiction, never historical biography.

See `../companions/PYOTTER-MIKWHALE.md`.

## Review test

Before accepting character writing or behavior, ask:

1. Is this visibly rooted in mutual aid and freedom from domination?
2. Is the market inversion sincere, pluralist, and anti-privilege rather than pro-corporate?
3. Does the friend remain free to ignore, refuse, or disagree?
4. Is the idea shown through adorable behavior before exposition?
5. Could anyone mistake this for a historical quotation or factual claim?
6. Does it preserve the no-economy, no-persuasion product boundary?

If the last two answers are yes and no respectively, revise it.
