# Certified separation in the local-seasonality sensitivity

24 September 2026. This is a numerical diagnosis after the first core-DV R3 fit failed, not a change selected for a p-value. The all-record specifications had fitted; the core-DV annual-seasonality specification encountered a singular information matrix. Preserve the failed attempt and this resolution alongside the frozen battery.

The aggregate panel contains two core-DV ZIPs with positive outcomes at only one annual phase: 92011 and 92110, one eligible group each. The same condition holds under the strict warrant mask. With ZIP-specific intercept, sine and cosine coefficients, these data exhibit exact Poisson separation.

For the ZIP's positive phase `theta0`, the direction `v(theta) = cos(theta-theta0)-1` is nonpositive at every date and zero at every positive outcome. Its components use only that ZIP's intercept, sine and cosine regressors. Moving indefinitely in this direction leaves the linear predictors for positive outcomes unchanged while driving means to zero on all other phases. The likelihood improves toward a finite supremum, but these nuisance coefficients do not have finite maximizing values. This mechanism is established by the data and geometry, not by a small-event-count exclusion threshold.

`diagnose_annual_separation.py` verifies the condition using exact rational phases `(day-of-year - 1) / days-in-year` and confirms that every excluded row has outcome zero. Its certificate is saved in `separation_diagnostic/diagnostic.json` and the companion CSV. For the unrestricted geographic panel, 1,460 zero rows are separated in 92011 and 1,458 in 92110: **2,918 zero rows; no positive outcomes removed**. The remaining phase support has one and three dates respectively. For the paired station panel, missing NOAA dates reduce the first ZIP's separated set to 1,435 rows, giving **2,893 separated zero rows** in total; both positive counts remain.

The principled resolution is the extended-MLE computation: remove only the certified separated zero rows, retain all rows at the supported phase, and remove the two annual-harmonic columns for each affected ZIP because on retained rows they are redundant with its ZIP intercept. Then fit the remaining full-rank model and verify its finite score, implied coefficient step, information matrix and exposure covariance. The temperature coefficient is an estimable direction, not part of the exhibited separating direction. A one-row ZIP is saturated by its intercept and supplies no within-ZIP exposure information.

Apply the same rule to every annual-seasonality specification and outcome. Do not remove an entire sparse ZIP merely because its total count is small, change knots or covariates, add a silent penalty, or relax the convergence tolerance. The reduced-row fit evaluates the same likelihood supremum for the estimable parameters, rather than defining a new exposure hypothesis. The planned 24-test family therefore remains intact. The original complete zero-inclusive descriptive panel is unchanged; fitted rows and parameter counts must separately identify the extended-MLE reduction.

This certificate detects one specific mechanism; it is not a proof that no other separating direction exists. If the reduced fit still fails, use a general certified separation procedure or report the failure conservatively. A successful finite, full-rank stationary solution and independent verification are still required.

The existence problem is documented by [Santos Silva and Tenreyro (2010), *Economics Letters* 107(2):310–312](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/TENREYRO/poissonpml.pdf). General high-dimensional separation and the conditions under which some parameter estimates remain estimable are developed by [Correia, Guimarães and Zylkin (2019), *Verifying the existence of maximum likelihood estimates for generalized linear models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01633). These references support handling separation explicitly; the exact circular-phase certificate above is specific to this dataset and design.
