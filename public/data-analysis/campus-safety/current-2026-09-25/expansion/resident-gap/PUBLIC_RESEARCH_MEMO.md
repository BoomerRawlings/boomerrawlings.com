# Public-university resident population follow-up

Research date: September 26, 2026. Scope: seven institutions with usable 2024 housing offense counts but no adopted housing population. Publication unchanged.

**Result: 25 qualified observations; no newly verified complete, same-year student-resident denominator.** This is a bounded source review, not proof that complete figures do not exist. The earlier generic missing label concealed useful partial data and acquisition gaps. The Florida finding also demonstrates that searching extracted text alone was insufficient.

| Institution | Strongest relevant observation | Why it cannot yet supply the complete denominator |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | Fiscal-year 2024 single-student housing occupancy: 8,941 | Single-student housing only; census date unspecified |
| Illinois Urbana-Champaign | Fall 2024 tenth-day residence halls: 9,898 contracts | Apartment students not fully counted; expanded off-campus contracts need reconciliation |
| Washington Seattle | Autumn 2024 broader housing series: approximately 9,044 students | Approximate operational series; separately managed housing not fully covered |

## Florida

The official [2026 investor presentation](https://cfo.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/FINAL-UF-2026-Investor-Presentation.pptx), slide 8, contains an **embedded image** with occupied counts that the earlier text extraction missed. Visual transcription: fiscal years 2022–2025 respectively 7,533, 8,089, 8,941 and 8,313. Blue labels are capacity; orange-bar labels are occupancy. The title limits this to single-student residences. Fiscal labels must not be silently relabeled fall census years.

The second avenue, [graduate/family housing terms](https://housing.ufl.edu/rates-payments-agreements/housing-agreements/graduate-and-family-housing-terms-and-conditions/), establishes student leaseholders but permits spouses and children; it does not give the missing count. Rates likewise do not give occupancy. A student-government resolution pointing to a master plan was indexed but returned 404 on original retrieval; its capacity claims were not adopted. A complete figure still requires same-period graduate/family student counts and geographic reconciliation.

## Illinois Urbana-Champaign

The [2024–2025 housing annual report](https://recruitment.housing.illinois.edu/sites/default/files/2025-08/FY24-25_Annual-Report_FINAL.pdf), PDF 5, supplies exact fall tenth-day hall observations for 2022–2024 and spring observations through 2025. PDF 18 defines fiscal-year unique residents as **including dependents**; 12,020 in FY2024 therefore cannot be substituted for a student census. PDF 4 separately describes 215 rented off-campus spaces, whose relation to hall contracts is unresolved.

The scanned [2023–2024 report](https://recruitment.housing.illinois.edu/sites/default/files/2024-11/Design%20Draft%20Annual%20Report%20-FY23-24-Low.pdf) was rendered because its text layer is empty. PDF 17 corroborates the historical hall counts but gives apartment occupancy as percentages and lease renewal measures. Its inherited footer says 2022–2023, while the cover and table describe 2023–2024; the table’s own periods govern. The public [resident eligibility policy](https://hsgintranet2024.web.illinois.edu/policy-resident-eligibility) also distinguishes enrolled hall contract holders from broader apartment affiliation. Apartment leases are not automatically student headcounts.

## Washington Seattle

The [FY2024 bondholder report](https://finance.uw.edu/treasury/files/BHReport/University%20of%20Washington%20Bondholders%20Report%20Fiscal%20Year%202024.pdf), PDF 94, gives 2022–2024 hall occupancy of 8,654, 8,515 and 8,439. Footnote 3 explicitly excludes single-student and family apartments. PDF 121 gives a broader autumn 2024 figure of approximately 9,044 students, compared with 9,614 in 2023; it discusses Commodore Duchess separately and records management changes affecting Nordheim and Radford.

The [current Housing & Food Services table](https://hfs.washington.edu/about-hfs/facts-figures/) gives 2025–2026 tenth-day occupancy of 9,149, explicitly excluding three named apartment groups. These exclusions are known missing components, not merely an unverified parcel match.

A fresh original-page retrieval of the [Treasury listing](https://finance.uw.edu/treasury/bondholders/other-investor-material) found a newer 2025 report, unlike the cached search result. Its [official EMMA disclosure](https://emma.msrb.org/MarketActivity/ContinuingDisclosureDetails/P11450426) requires a terms-acceptance gate; the coordinator did not accept it. No university-hosted mirror was verified. Thus the newest report is **listed but not acquired**, and the acquisition gap remains open.

## Wisconsin Madison

The official [August 28, 2024 move-in article](https://news.wisc.edu/follow-that-cart/) reports 8,915 residents and expressly marks its figures approximate. It describes hall move-in; it does not establish the full apartment student population. The current [Housing description](https://www.housing.wisc.edu/about/) separately describes apartment communities serving students, families, researchers and employees, so a general apartment-resident count would not necessarily be student-only.

The university-commissioned [2024 housing market study](https://news.wisc.edu/content/uploads/2024/09/Madison-Report-082624.pdf) was also reviewed; operating capacity is not occupied student headcount. A deeper third avenue used the [official sustainability dashboard](https://sustainability.wisc.edu/sustainability-dashboard/operations/): public CSV exports and the downloadable energy workbook were acquired. They contain weighted campus-user totals, not the underlying student-resident component. The stored workbook schema confirms that absence in this particular export; reversing the normalization would be unjustified. The sustainability report does not close the housing gap.

## Ohio State Columbus

The official [August 16, 2024 move-in article](https://news.osu.edu/president-carter-joins-move-in-day-effort-to-welcome-students-back-to-campus/) expects more than 15,000 students in Columbus halls after the weekend. This is useful scale context, not an exact completed census.

The follow-up also retrieved the [FY2025 financial report](https://busfin.osu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/Ohio_State_Financial_Report_2025-accessible_0.pdf) through the official financial listing and inspected housing/residence-hall references; no occupied-student table was found. Earlier [Student Life impact reporting](https://studentlife.osu.edu/documents/sl-impact-report-2024-2025-tagged.pdf) and the bondholder update were additional operational/financial avenues. Neither supplied a verified complete count. The remaining gap is incomplete acquisition/verification, not an established absence of administrative data.

## Penn State University Park

The university-hosted [2023–2024 Parents & Families Guide](https://newkensington.psu.edu/files/pdf/15116/2024/02/14/pp-parentsandfamiliesguide-accessible-compressed.pdf), PDF 34, gives explicit lower bounds of more than 14,500 undergraduate and more than 300 graduate residents. Their sum supports **more than 14,800**, not an exact count. The adjacent section explicitly concerns 2023 move-in; a URL upload date in 2024 is not a fall 2024 census.

The [2024 security report](https://www.police.psu.edu/sites/police/files/2024-11/asr-penn-state-university-park-2024a.pdf), printed 64, defines actual occupancy but supplies a table labeled capacity. The [Student Affairs 2023–2024 overview](https://studentaffairs.psu.edu/sites/default/files/StudentAffairs_AnnualReportOverview_2023-2024.pdf) gives an approximate Commonwealth-campus housing population, which does not belong to University Park. The [sustainability listing](https://sustainability.psu.edu/campus-efforts/by-the-numbers/rankings-ratings/) links a 2023 STARS submission; no additional public full-report mirror was verified during this follow-up.

## Virginia

The coordinator recovered the original [2025 Student Affairs annual report](https://studentaffairs.virginia.edu/sites/studentaffairs/files/2026-02/UVA_StudentAffairs_2025AnnualReport_020226.pdf) through the [official February 11, 2026 listing](https://studentaffairs.virginia.edu/communications/2026/student-affairs-2025-annual-report), resolving the ordinary-client 403 acquisition failure. PDF 12–14 was inspected, including a visual review of PDF 14. It states fall occupancy 99% and spring 96% for the 2024–2025 activity period, without an actual resident total. This is a successful acquisition but no new denominator.

The December 2024 finance-book avenue still returned 403. Earlier board material gave bed inventory and projected occupancy; multiplying rounded percentages by bed counts does not yield an exact headcount. The missing student total remains unverified rather than proved nonexistent.

## Reuse and verification

- `qualified_observations.json`: 25 records with values, periods, precision, reasons against rate use, source URLs/pages and full SHA-256 hashes. `proposed_population_observations.csv` is the parallel table. Every record explicitly sets `eligible_for_rate=false`.
- `population_additions.csv`: header only; no eligible complete denominator proposed.
- `SOURCE_MANIFEST.json`: raw-source metadata, preserved original retrieval dates and successful/failed acquisition outcomes. Original bytes for all qualified observations are local in `sources/`. The Virginia PDF recovery retains its earlier failed-attempt note.
- `FINDINGS_AUDIT.json`: hash and observation checks. `renders/` and Florida’s `slide8_image17.png` preserve visual-review evidence. Source URLs, not local machine paths, are the citations.
- No capacity substituted for occupants; no dependent-inclusive annual population presented as students; no fiscal-year, spring or guide-period value backfilled as fall. Known partial counts require a compatible narrower numerator before rates could be defensible. These rules also allow genuinely whole-institution student counts with an explicit geographic-proxy caveat, consistently with earlier accepted observations.
