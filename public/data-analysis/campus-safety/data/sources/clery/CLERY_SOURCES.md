# Federal Clery source acquisition and definitions

Snapshot checked September 25, 2026. This acquisition is separate from the completed crime-and-heat study. No university rates or rankings were calculated here.

## Source and version

The official [download catalog](https://ope.ed.gov/campussafety/api/dataFiles/fileList) and [available collection years](https://ope.ed.gov/campussafety/api/dataFiles/years) list 2025 as the latest public collection, covering calendar reporting years 2022–2024. `Crime2025EXCEL.zip` contains 29 spreadsheets, 29 Word codebooks and a ReadMe. The ReadMe states it was prepared April 27, 2026; this is a file-preparation date, not an independently verified publication date. One ReadMe line mistakenly names `Crime2024EXCEL.zip`; catalog, archive filename and table year fields consistently identify the 2025 collection. [Official 2025 download](https://ope.ed.gov/campussafety/api/dataFiles/file?fileName=Crime2025EXCEL.zip).

The [download page's versioning statement](https://ope.ed.gov/campussafety/public/html/datafile/datafile.list.html) explains that each collection contains three prior calendar years and the frozen bulk files do not include corrections after that collection closes. All 11 Excel archives for collections 2015–2025 were acquired, totaling approximately 444 MB plus instructions/catalogs. These retain overlapping 2012–2024 reporting years; only the latest 2025 collection is normalized for the frozen primary 2022–2024 analysis. Historical outcomes were not spliced across vintages.

The public `source_metadata_primary_2025.json` records exact URLs, acquisition/verification timing, file lengths and SHA-256 hashes for the primary archive and instructions, plus ZIP-member hashes. A separate internal historical manifest is not part of the primary release. Source files remain unchanged. Latest archive: 38,213,941 bytes, SHA-256 `28143ab29f9f9d43235800a3f1f4061b8f2928a7d11bce0e8327d2889ab6f007`.

## Unit and geography

The 2025 on-campus table contains 10,111 campus records. The protocol's 42 named institutions match 212 federal campus records: 165 in the United States and 47 outside it according to the official API's geographic flag. Each campus reports separately, including campuses sharing an IPEDS identifier. Additional campuses are defined by institutional control, geographic separation, an organized program of study and on-site administrative capacity. Shared campuses can be reported by more than one Title IV institution. Therefore, summing across universities does not necessarily enumerate geographically unique offenses. [2025 survey guide, printed pp. 2–3; PDF pp. 5–6](https://surveys.ope.ed.gov/csss2025/wwwroot/documents/Campus_Safety_Users_Guide.pdf#page=5).

`UNITID_P` is labeled `Unitid_plus` in the codebook. For this cohort, removal of the final three digits is validated against all 42 IPEDS UNITID/OPEID pairs and the official API institution identifiers for all 212 campuses. OPEID alone is not a safe institutional grouping key: distinct IPEDS units can share it. `identifier_validation.json` documents these checks. `cohort_campus_crosswalk_2025.csv` retains exact campus identifiers, institution labels, branch labels and locations. No physical-campus or housing selection is inferred from the first branch suffix.

Institution-wide counts retain medical centers, field stations and international branches associated with the selected federal institution. Examples include Harvard's Longwood and international branches, Yale's West Campus, five UCSF branches and SDSU's Imperial Valley and Georgia branches. These are not automatically equivalent to an intuitively defined main campus. `cohort_geography_scope_2025.csv` and `cohort_campus_context_current.json` make the aggregation explicit. [Official example: Harvard institution detail](https://ope.ed.gov/campussafety/api/institution/166027); [official example: UCSD institution detail](https://ope.ed.gov/campussafety/api/institution/110680).

The five source geographies are on campus; on-campus student housing facilities; noncampus property; public property; and unallocated statistics reported by local/state police. Student housing is a subset of on-campus geography and must not be added to it. Noncampus is an ownership/control and educational-use definition, not all crime in a surrounding neighborhood. [Guide, printed pp. 25, 28 and 31; PDF pp. 28, 31 and 34](https://surveys.ope.ed.gov/csss2025/wwwroot/documents/Campus_Safety_Users_Guide.pdf#page=28).

The local/state-police table applies when a crime is known to fall within Clery geography but cannot be assigned to its exact category. It is not a second count of all police-reported crimes. All corresponding cohort cells are blank in the 2025 bulk file. This table is retained separately, never added to on-campus counts. [Guide, printed p. 40; PDF p. 43](https://surveys.ope.ed.gov/csss2025/wwwroot/documents/Campus_Safety_Users_Guide.pdf#page=43).

## Outcomes and reporting time

The criminal-offense table has 11 categories: murder/nonnegligent manslaughter; negligent manslaughter; rape; fondling; incest; statutory rape; robbery; aggravated assault; burglary; motor vehicle theft; arson. A descriptive sum must be labeled as the sum of these listed reported offense categories. It is not a count of unique incidents or victims, and it is not all possible crimes. Separate VAWA, hate, arrest and disciplinary-referral families must remain distinct. [Guide, printed pp. 28–29; PDF pp. 31–32](https://surveys.ope.ed.gov/csss2025/wwwroot/documents/Campus_Safety_Users_Guide.pdf#page=31).

Report year is the year the crime was reported, which can differ from occurrence year. Attempted and completed crimes are generally included, subject to the stated homicide exception. VAWA contains domestic violence, dating violence and stalking. Hate-crime totals and their bias breakdowns are classifications, not additional offenses to add to the criminal-offense family. [Guide, printed pp. 28, 43 and 58–59; PDF pp. 31, 46 and 61–62](https://surveys.ope.ed.gov/csss2025/wwwroot/documents/Campus_Safety_Users_Guide.pdf#page=31).

Arrests and referrals each contain weapons, drug and liquor law violations. Referrals concern violations of law, not every breach of institutional policy; when a person is both arrested and referred for the same incident, the instructions require counting the arrest only. Unfounded crimes are separately reported and excluded from the crime/VAWA/hate counts; only sworn or commissioned law-enforcement personnel may make that determination under the stated conditions. [Guide, printed pp. 82 and 97; PDF pp. 85 and 100](https://surveys.ope.ed.gov/csss2025/wwwroot/documents/Campus_Safety_Users_Guide.pdf#page=85).

## Enrollment and missingness

The bulk metadata contain one `men_total`, `women_total` and `Total` triplet, not separate denominators for 2022–2024. The codebook labels these only as total men, total women and grand total. Across the 42 institutions, the triplet's total matches independently acquired IPEDS fall-2024 enrollment, and it repeats across each institution's campus rows. It must not be summed across branches or reused as all three annual denominators. Annual enrollment is supplied separately by the enrollment acquisition. `identifier_validation.json` points to the verified crosswalk; it does not claim campus-specific enrollment.

The `FILTER22/23/24` codebook labels read only `Data_year = YYYY (FILTER)`. They do not establish whether zero means nonoperation, newly reported geography, or another absence. In this cohort every FILTER=0 count is blank. Those cells remain unavailable; no zero is fabricated. Housing and noncampus blanks can also arise from survey screening. The guide instructs each campus to answer geography screening for the designated calendar year; campuses without the geography do not complete its screen. A current declaration is not automatically evidence for earlier years. See `COVERAGE.md`. [Guide, printed pp. 1 and 25; PDF pp. 4 and 28](https://surveys.ope.ed.gov/csss2025/wwwroot/documents/Campus_Safety_Users_Guide.pdf#page=28).

## Revisions and source discrepancies

The 2025 guide allows the 2023 table to be corrected but says 2022 changes must be recorded in the text caveat rather than editing that table. Hence a caveat or an ASR can state a corrected oldest-year value while the bulk cell retains the prior value. This is not proof that the ASR is newer than the bulk's preparation date. [Guide, printed p. 28 and Appendix B p. 114; PDF pp. 31 and 117](https://surveys.ope.ed.gov/csss2025/wwwroot/documents/Campus_Safety_Users_Guide.pdf#page=31).

An independent comparison of UCSD, UCSC and SDSU main-campus ASR rape cells found 34 of 36 matches; UCSD 2022 on-campus and noncampus cells differ by one. The fixed federal cells remain primary. The ASR audit and source-specific explanatory comparisons are in the separate research folder. Current federal API caveats independently document the UCSD corrections. `cohort_context_notes_current.json` retains the explanatory table notes, labeled as a separate API snapshot; it does not replace source counts.

## Reproduction and publication

Requires Python 3.10+, xlrd 2.0.2 and openpyxl; olefile 0.47 was used only to inspect the original Word codebooks. Run `acquire_primary_clery.py`, `build_crosswalk.py` and `normalize_clery.py`. `audit_identifiers.py` additionally uses the separately acquired enrollment crosswalk and the supplied sanitized API snapshot. `derive_residential_2024.py` makes the documented 2024-only applicability aggregate. No source-derivation step calculates a rate or ranking. The optional `acquire_context.py` re-queries mutable official context; a later response is a new vintage, not an automatic replacement for the supplied snapshot. See `README.md` for the frozen reproduction sequence and publication exclusions.

Publish curated normalized counts, campus/institution crosswalks, source URLs/hashes and methodology. Raw campus API responses contain public staff-contact fields; they are retained locally for provenance and must not be packaged. Normalized context includes only campus geography, public explanatory notes and source metadata. National raw ZIPs and historical extracts can remain local with official upstream download links. No individual crime records are present in these bulk tables.
