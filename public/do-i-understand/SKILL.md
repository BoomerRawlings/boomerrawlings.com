# Do I Understand?

Find out what the user can explain and apply, not what they recognize when they see the answer. This is a formative check, not a grade, a proof of mastery, or a test of who originated an idea.

1. **Pick a target.** From the conversation or material the user points to, name one specific concept, claim, or decision to test. Ask what to test only if it's unclear. For code, target behavior or a design choice, not syntax trivia. If the underlying answer isn't reliably known, say so and narrow the check or use an explicitly hypothetical case.
2. **Ask for a teach-back.** Ask one neutral question that requires explaining why or how in the user's own words. Don't summarize the answer, offer choices, or plant the key reasoning in the question. Wait for the answer. Accept terse, informal, or differently worded responses that show the mechanism.
3. **Test a fresh case.** Change one assumption or condition that matters. Ask what they predict and why; wait for their attempt. Don't just substitute different names or numbers into the original example. Don't reveal whether their first answer was correct before this check unless they ask for help.
4. **Report the evidence.** In a few lines, name what they demonstrated, what is still untested or mistaken, and one next step. Cite something they actually said. Separate an unaided attempt from an assisted one. A correct guess without reasoning, a missing answer, or a single example is not proof of mastery. Don't infer confidence, authorship, or novelty from performance.

If they ask for a hint, give the smallest useful hint and mark that attempt assisted. If they ask for an explanation instead, give it and offer a different case afterward; don't count repeating the explanation as independent evidence. “I don't know” is a valid result. Keep the default to two questions, one at a time; probe further only when useful and welcome.

## Boundaries

Use conversation and user-selected materials. Don't edit files, install anything, execute code, upload answers, or collect telemetry as part of the check. Don't assume a private answer can be published. For contested or open questions, assess whether reasoning follows from the stated assumptions, not agreement with the model.
